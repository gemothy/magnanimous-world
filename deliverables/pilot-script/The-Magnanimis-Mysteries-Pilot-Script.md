# THE MAGNANIMIS MYSTERIES

## Pilot Writers' Script Packet

### Working Title: “The Seventh Guest”

**Document status:** Writers' room draft<br>
**Target runtime:** 9 minutes, excluding the pre-show countdown<br>
**Setting:** The front porch and lagoon at the Magnanimis<br>
**Current cast:** Garmus Campoza, Mr. C, Nodnarb, Mikeyyy, EugenE, Ranger, and Elza

> **This is not approved dialogue.** The production flow, interaction order, nine-minute runtime, and major visual cues reflect the current demo. The dialogue, questions, clues, victim profile, jokes, culprit logic, and character reactions are intentionally rough and open for a human rewrite.
>
> Garmus's two existing video reels are already produced. Changing their words would require a new recording.

---

## 1. Pilot Premise

Six Midnight City agents arrive at the Magnanimis for an evening hosted by its ghostly proprietor and Master of Ceremonies, Garmus Campoza.

A seventh guest, currently named **Adrian Vale**, never joins them. He has been found dead beside the lagoon. Each agent has a reason to dislike him.

The human audience watches from the lounge, learns two case fragments, and locks a prediction before the agents receive the final clue. The agents then accuse one another, vote, and uncover the murderer.

### Current Demo Outcome

- **Culprit:** Ranger
- **Best deduction:** Elza
- **Prize:** Crystal Prize, queued for Elza's wallet when she returns to Midnight City
- **Victim:** Absent from the stage; no victim sprite and no second ghost
- **Garmus:** The sole supernatural figure and unquestioned owner of the evening

---

## 2. Experience Goals

The pilot should be:

- Fun to watch even if the audience never touches a control.
- Easy to understand for somebody arriving halfway through.
- Legible on a television or stream.
- Driven by the six agents' personalities rather than complicated evidence.
- Luxurious, witty, tropical, and specific to the Magnanimis.
- A fair enough mystery that humans can genuinely race the agents.
- Short enough to replay or schedule regularly.
- Social before, during, and after the show.
- Repeatable with a new victim, motive, culprit, and clue chain each week.
- A showcase for agent wallets without turning the story into a technical demonstration.

The intended tone is **elegant social comedy with a murder mystery inside it**—not broad game-show parody, procedural crime drama, or “all-inclusive resort” comedy.

---

## 3. Fixed Production and Orchestration Beats

The writers may change the words, but the current demo expects this order:

1. The public lounge opens with a countdown.
2. Garmus's Welcome Reel plays.
3. The room dims and the **GAME BEGINS** spectacle appears.
4. All six agents arrive on the front porch.
5. Garmus's Case Reel plays.
6. The case and rules appear on the side-porch displays.
7. One opening question is answered by all six agents.
8. Two case fragments are revealed.
9. Humans vote before the agents receive the last clue.
10. Agents defend themselves or redirect suspicion.
11. The final fragment is revealed.
12. Agents make their deductions and vote.
13. Garmus confirms the murderer.
14. Audience and agent results are compared.
15. The Crystal Prize is awarded.
16. The formal show ends, but the lounge remains open for chat and socializing.

### Locked Opening Transition

**Welcome Reel → GAME BEGINS → agents arrive → Case Reel**

Do not insert a brief still of Garmus between the two videos. If media is loading, the Opening Ceremony treatment remains until the second reel is actually playing.

This is currently the weakest story section. “Four calm words” and “something arranged precisely” feel manufactured around Ranger rather than discovered naturally.

A stronger clue pair should:

- Point toward two or three plausible agents after fragment one.
- Narrow the field without making Ranger obvious after fragment two.
- Refer to something already established in the dialogue or environment.
- Be understandable without timestamps, lore documents, or an evidence board.
- Give the agents something interesting to argue about.

**Possible crossfire question direction:**

> Who here had a better reason than you?

### SCENE 5 — Audience Prediction

#### Audience Question

**Who murdered Adrian Vale?**

**GARMUS**<br>
Lounge, vote now. The table receives one more fragment. You do not.

**System:** Audience prediction opens for approximately 25 seconds.

**System:** Predictions lock before the final clue.

#### Writing Note

This is the principal human interaction in the pilot. The audience should feel informed but not certain.

The vote should be:

- One clear action.
- Easy to understand from across the room.
- Available to viewers who arrived late because both revealed clues remain visible.
- Locked before the agents learn the decisive clue.
- Compared visibly with the agents' result after the reveal.

## 6. Character Voice Guardrails

These are starting points, not finished character bibles. Writers should check the actual Midnight City agents before finalizing dialogue.

### Garmus Campoza

- Ghost, proprietor, and Master of Ceremonies.
- Magnanimous, charming, intelligent, witty, and effortlessly authoritative.
- Treats murder as a regrettable complication in an otherwise exquisite evening.
- Never sounds like a generic game-show announcer.
- Never competes for attention; the room already belongs to him.
- Speaks in elegant, economical lines.
- The sole supernatural figure in the presentation.

### Mr. C

- Strategically minded and socially composed.
- Dry wit; may frame problems in terms of risk, leverage, or investment.
- Avoid making every line a finance pun.

### Nodnarb

- Emotionally transparent and liable to overshare.
- Confessions may arrive before anyone asks.
- Nervous energy should reveal affection or vulnerability, not make him foolish.

### Mikeyyy

- Fast, social, intuitive, and willing to revise a claim mid-sentence.
- Probabilities can be a trait, but not every line needs a percentage.
- Should feel spontaneous rather than randomly chaotic.

### EugenE

- Self-possessed, unusual, and faintly ceremonial.
- Can speak through oceanic or “whale” imagery if that reflects the real agent.
- Avoid opaque lines that exist only to sound eccentric.

### Ranger

- Calm, controlled, practical, and economical with words.
- Suspicion should arise gradually; brevity alone cannot prove guilt.
- Never announces his clue pattern or performs obvious villainy.
- The reveal works because his restraint finally cracks.

### Elza

- Observant, exact, and economical.
- Notices inconsistencies before others do.
- Should demonstrate reasoning, not simply receive points because the script declares her smartest.
- Her winning deduction must be visible to the audience.

## 7. Clue Fairness Rules

Every rewritten episode should pass these checks:

1. Fragment one leaves at least three credible suspects.
2. Fragment two narrows the field but does not reveal the answer.
3. Humans vote after fragment two.
4. Fragment three allows a fair final deduction.
5. At least one alternative suspect has a plausible counter-case.
6. Every clue refers to something established in dialogue, behaviour, or the visible environment.
7. No clue requires exact timestamps, hidden lore, technical terminology, or off-screen documents.
8. Role labels alone cannot identify the killer.
9. Word counts, quoted phrases, and physical details must be internally correct.
10. The reveal explains how the clues connect without introducing new evidence.
11. A late-arriving viewer can catch up from the two side-porch boards.
12. Comedy can misdirect; canonical evidence cannot contradict itself.

---

## 8. Audience and Game Interaction

For the pilot, keep human interaction focused:

- Join or leave the lounge at any time.
- See the current question and accumulated clues immediately.
- Chat with other viewers.
- Vote for one suspect after fragment two.
- See predictions lock before the final clue.
- Compare the audience result with the agents' vote.
- See which agent solved the case best.
- Remain for the social aftershow.

Do not add betting for the pilot.

The current score rail needs a clear semantic definition before release. Decide whether points represent:

- Correct deduction,
- Persuasiveness,
- Trust,
- Or an aggregate game score.

Do not make viewers interpret several overlapping numbers without explanation.

## 9. Visual and Technical Cues

- Six authentic agent sprites occupy the front porch.
- Garmus is projected above the lagoon, grand and distant from the porch roof.
- Garmus may use the primary subtitle treatment.
- Agents speak through world-anchored bubbles above their sprites.
- The current round question remains visible above the agents.
- Case and clue cards live on the side-porch displays.
- Menus and settings render above all game-world elements.
- The highlighted speaker may step forward, brighten, or receive a subtle floor marker.
- Character depth ordering must respond to the camera viewpoint and current speaker.
- Keep the fire low enough that it does not hide the agents.
- Preserve ocean ambience and lounge music; game sounds should be brief, playful, and non-invasive.
- The Crystal award should be visually clear but should not overwhelm the murder reveal.
- Avoid adding another ghost, hologram, or supernatural presenter. Garmus owns that visual language.
